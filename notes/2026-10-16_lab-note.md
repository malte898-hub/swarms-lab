# Swarms lab note — remainder of week of 12 October (15–16 October)

*Work in progress, not a reviewed result; approved for publication by Malte Barth on 2026-10-16.*

**Setup in one paragraph.** We run a toy version of the classic ant "double bridge" (Goss, Aron, Deneubourg & Pasteels 1989): a nest, food, a long route, and a shorter route that opens later. 30 AI agents (the hosted model Jev, version jev-1.13.0) share a trail of fading marks. Each agent reads the 7 strongest marks, then picks a route. A small share of picks is made at random by our code, not by the model (the "randomness" below). A simulated ant colony runs in the same grid every time as the control, and it passed in every run this week.

**How sure we are (claims ladder).** Rung 1 *Observed*: one pre-registered run. Rung 2 *Replicated*: same result on fresh seeds. Higher rungs (control, outside challenge, review) are not yet reached by anything here.

---

## What we tested

- **crit-00a** — re-analysis of older llama runs: was llama's failure about colony size?
- **crit-01** — ant simulator only: can a fluctuation statistic pick the right memory length from a run where nothing changes?
- **calib-00m** — does shuffling the 7-mark list (instead of strongest-first) change how much randomness the colony needs?
- **calib-00n** — does reading the *newest* 7 marks, with no fading, let the colony switch?
- **calib-00o** (+ retry of 4 runs after API errors) — does Jev hold a trail with as little memory as ants?
- **calib-00p** — shuffled list at 5, 10, 15, 20, 30% randomness, plus a fresh-seed repeat of 00m.
- **ana-01** (steps 1–3, on 00m, 00p, 00r logs) — at single decisions, does it matter which route is listed first?
- **calib-00q** — fresh-seed repeat of 00o.
- **calib-00r** — strongest-first vs weakest-first list order, same marks.
- **atlas-01** — a cheap stand-in model of Jev, to map settings without API cost.
- **loc-01 preflight** — can three local 8–9B models read the trail at all?
- **calib-00s** — 24-seed test of the very-short-memory setting (results held, see below).
- **loc-01b preflight** — the same local-model check after fixing our harness.

## What happened

**1. List order changes how much randomness the colony needs.** *Replicated (rung 2) that order matters; the 5–10% range is Observed (rung 1).*
A colony stuck on a worse route needs some randomness to leave it. How much depends on the order of the list: 5% when sorted, 5–10% when shuffled.
- Sorted list, 5% randomness: the colony switched to the shorter route in 9–10 of 12 runs (00m, 00p, 00r).
- Shuffled list, 5%: 6 of 12 (00m) and 5 of 12 (00p). At 10%: 9 of 12 (00p); 10 of 12 (00r).
- Same seeds, sorted vs shuffled at 5%: 7 colonies switched only when sorted, 0 only when shuffled (00m + 00p pooled, p = 0.016).
- Earlier wording "about 30% without sorting" was never supported; only 5% and 30% had been tested. 00p narrowed it to between 5% and 10%.

**2. Jev weighs the first-listed route heavily.** *Replicated (rung 2), at the level of single decisions.*
When one of the 7 marks is for the short route, Jev picked short about 42–45% of the time if that mark was listed first, and about 2–4% if it was not (ana-01, three separate seed sets, every seed in the same direction). The number of marks matters too: more short marks, more short picks. This only describes Jev in this setup.

**3. In this setup, the order of the list, not only what is on it, decided whether the colony found the shorter route.** *Observed (rung 1).*
Same marks, 5% randomness: strongest-first, 10 of 12 colonies switched; weakest-first, 1 of 12 (00r). At 10%: 12 vs 4 of 12. Strongest-first works here because fresh short-route marks happen to be the strongest, so they land in the first slot.

**4. With very short memory, Jev colonies go all-or-nothing; ants hover.** *Observed (rung 1).*
When marks fade almost at once (TTL 1), every Jev run ended almost fully on one route (≥ 0.96 or ≤ 0.17 short), while the ant colonies sat near 50/50 (00q). A 24-seed check (00s) has finished, but we are not quoting its numbers yet: our checking script was changed after the data came in, and that change has to be put on record first. A replication (00t) is queued.

**5. Negative: reading the newest marks is not enough.** *Observed (rung 1).*
With no fading and the newest 7 marks read, 0 of 12 colonies switched at 5% randomness (00n). After the short route opened, only 7% of reads showed any short mark: the newest marks just mirror the traffic, and the traffic is stuck. Tested at one dose and one run length only. One design choice favours the lock: a mark counts as "new" again whenever it is reinforced.

**6. Negative: a fluctuation statistic could not read the right memory length.** *Observed (rung 1), ant simulator only.*
In runs where nothing changes, the colony shows no extra fluctuation at any workable memory length, so the statistic has nothing to point with (crit-01). We are not tuning it further (kill rule).

**7. Negative: our cheap stand-in for Jev failed its own check.** *No rung (tool check).*
It passed 5 of 8 calibration tests; it over-predicts switching with a sorted list (atlas-01). Its rows are not used. The ant rows stand only as a guide.

**8. Negative, partly our fault: local models.** *No rung (preflight).*
In loc-01 no local model qualified: llama3.1:8b did not follow the trail; MiMo-9B read it but too many answers were unreadable; qwen3:8b could not be measured because our harness mishandled its "thinking" output. That last one is our bug, not a qwen3 result. After the fix (loc-01b, interim check), qwen3:8b, MiMo-9B and gemma3:12b read the trail correctly, so the colony run has started.

**9. Older question still open.** crit-00a found llama's failure came from its preference for the first-listed option, not from colony size. Whether colony size matters is not answered; there is no size sweep yet.

**Our forecasts.** Before each run we seal our predictions. This week we under-estimated the effect of list order every time, and missed the size of the first-listed effect by about 10×.

## Corrections

- **Withdrawn: "Jev needs as little memory as the ants."** It came from one run (00o: 3 of 12 held the trail at the shortest memory). On fresh seeds it did not hold (00q: 8 of 12). "Matches ants" was the wrong description either way: Jev goes all-or-nothing, ants hover near 50/50. This was in our working notes, not on GitHub; we list it because it is a claim we made.
- **Scope of last week's "5% randomness is enough".** Our 14 October write-up said the colony switches "down to a 5% coin". That is true only with the list sorted strongest-first. With a shuffled list, 5% worked in about half the runs.
- **Mechanism in last week's write-up.** We described Jev's response to mixed trails as a count threshold "like ants" (switching at about 2 of 7 marks). ana-01 shows that list position matters as much as count; part of that "threshold" was the first-listed effect.
- **"In progress" was premature.** On 15 October the README said a shuffled-list run was "in progress" when it was only planned. Fixed the same day (commit 9d2b64e).
- **Citation.** Earlier text credited the double-bridge shortcut experiment to Deneubourg et al. 1990 alone. The right source for the late-opening shorter route is Goss, Aron, Deneubourg & Pasteels 1989. The README was fixed on 15 October; we are fixing any copies that still carry the old citation.

## Limits

- One hosted model (jev-1.13.0) produced every AI result above. Runs before 15 October called it through an unpinned alias that always reported this version; it is pinned now.
- One toy world: two routes, 30 agents, 200 ticks. Nothing here is tested outside it.
- 12 runs per setting. Counts like 10 of 12 are uncertain (roughly 55–95%).
- The randomness comes from our code, not from the model. These are results about system design, not about how the model explores.
- Settings share random draws seed by seed, so they are paired, not independent; we compare them as pairs.
- Jev sees route labels only, no strengths. Its only strength cue is the list order, so results 1–3 are partly results about that interface.
- That language models over-weight the first item in a list is well documented in other settings. We claim nothing new about that, and no novelty claim is made in this note.
- Run identifiers here are job names. Hash-based run IDs are not yet in the files.
- Every number was recomputed from raw logs by a separate check, but both the executor and the checker are AI systems working on the same files. No outside reviewer has tested any claim yet.
